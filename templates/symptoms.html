Panic attack or heart attack? A quick symptom guide

Chest tightness, a racing heart, shortness of breath — panic attacks and heart attacks can feel a lot alike, which is exactly why so many people end up unsure and scared in the moment. This page lays out the common patterns of each side by side. It's educational only, not a diagnosis.

When in doubt, get checked out. If you have chest pain or pressure that is severe, crushing, spreading to your arm/jaw/back, or paired with fainting, cold sweat, or severe shortness of breath — call your local emergency number right away. It's always the safer choice to let a doctor rule out a heart problem rather than wait it out alone. This is true even if you've had panic attacks before — a new or different kind of chest pain is worth getting checked.

Panic attack — common symptoms

  • Sudden wave of intense fear or dread, often for no clear reason
  • Racing or pounding heart
  • Chest tightness or pain, usually sharp or localized rather than crushing
  • Shortness of breath or a feeling of being smothered
  • Trembling, shaking, or dizziness
  • Sweating, chills, or hot flashes
  • Nausea or a knotted stomach
  • Tingling or numbness, often in the hands or face
  • A feeling of unreality or being detached from yourself
  • Fear of losing control or "going crazy"

Panic attack symptoms typically peak within about 10 minutes and ease off within 20–30 minutes, though the fear it will happen again can linger.

Heart attack — common symptoms

  • Pressure, squeezing, fullness, or crushing pain in the center of the chest
  • Pain that spreads to the arm (often the left), shoulder, jaw, neck, or back
  • Chest discomfort that builds gradually or comes and goes over minutes
  • Shortness of breath, with or without chest discomfort
  • Cold sweat
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Lightheadedness or fainting
  • Unusual fatigue (sometimes days beforehand, more often reported by women)

Heart attack symptoms often build with exertion and don't fully ease with rest, unlike a panic attack. Some heart attacks — especially in women, older adults, and people with diabetes — show up with subtler symptoms and no chest pain at all.

Why they're so easy to mix up

Both involve the body's stress response, so a racing heart, chest tightness, sweating, and breathlessness show up in both. A few things can help you and a doctor tell them apart, but none of them are a substitute for being examined:

  • Trigger: panic attacks are sometimes tied to a stressful thought or situation, though they can also come out of nowhere; heart attack symptoms are more often tied to physical exertion or come on with no clear emotional trigger.
  • Pain quality: panic-related chest pain is often described as sharp or stabbing; heart attack pain is more often described as pressure or squeezing.
  • Duration: panic attacks tend to peak fast and fade within half an hour; heart attack symptoms often build gradually and persist.
  • Response to rest: panic attack symptoms usually ease as you calm down; heart attack symptoms often don't fully resolve with rest alone.

If you're ever genuinely unsure which one you're dealing with, treat it as a possible heart attack until a medical professional says otherwise — it's the safer assumption, and emergency staff would always rather check and find nothing than have someone wait through the real thing.

Frequent panic attacks can be exhausting and frightening to live with. If they're a regular part of your life, it's worth talking to a doctor or therapist — panic disorder is very treatable, and you don't have to just manage it on your own.
